The Hook

Have You Been Pwned?

A data breach is when a company gets hacked and the passwords of everyone who used it spill onto the internet.

The site haveibeenpwned.com lets you type your email and see exactly which breaches you've turned up in. Most people are shocked.

Attacks 101

How Passwords Get Cracked

01

Brute Force

A computer simply tries every possible combination until one works — billions of guesses per second.

aaaa → aaab → aaac …

02

Dictionary Attack

Instead of random guesses, it runs through real words and common passwords first — because people are predictable.

password · 123456 · qwerty

03

Credential Stuffing

They take a password leaked from one site and try it everywhere else, betting you reused it.

same login → 100 sites

Low-Tech Attacks

You Don't Need to Be a Hacker

Social Engineering

Tricking a person into handing over their password — voluntarily.

› Fake IT call asking for your password

› Phishing email → fake login page

› “Verify now or lose your account”

The most effective attacks never touch a computer.

Dumpster Diving

Digging through the trash for written passwords and personal clues.

› Sticky notes & printed lists

› Old phones, laptops, drives

› Pet names & birthdays on paper

What you throw away can be used against you.

Shoulder Surfing

Watching you type — or reading your screen, desk, and notes.

› Watching you type in public

› Notes stuck to a monitor

› A quick photo of your desk

Your environment is part of your security.

None of these require any technical skill.

Vocabulary

Know the Difference

Password

A secret string of characters you type to log in.

EXAMPLE

Tk8$rP!2

Passphrase

Several random words strung together — long but easy to remember.

EXAMPLE

honey-robot-jungle-sock

PIN

A short numeric code, usually unlocking one device.

EXAMPLE

4 8 2 7

Passkey

A login tied to your device — nothing to type, nothing to steal.

EXAMPLE

Face ID / fingerprint

WHY IS SMS WEAKEST?

SIM swapping.

An attacker calls your phone carrier, pretends to be you, and convinces them to move your number to their phone.

Now every SMS code comes straight to the attacker — no hacking required.

Under the Hood

How Passkeys Actually Work

TRADITIONAL PASSWORD LOGIN

1

You type your password

2

It travels across the internet to the server

3

Server checks it against a stored hash → allow / deny

⚠ Your credential leaves your device. If the server is breached, your password hash is exposed.

PASSKEY LOGIN

1

Site sends a random challenge to your device

2

Your device confirms it's you — Face ID, fingerprint, or PIN

3

Device signs the challenge with your private key

4

Server verifies it with your public key

5

Access granted — no password ever sent or stored

✓ Your private key never leaves your device. Breach the server and there's nothing useful to steal.

🔑

A passkey is a two-part math lock. The website holds one half; your device holds the other. Neither half is useful without the other.

The Tool

Let a Robot Remember Them

A password manager is an app that creates a long, random, unique password for every account — and remembers all of them for you.

You only remember one. It fills in the rest.

Bitwarden — free Your browser — built in

“BUT IF SOMEONE GETS MY MASTER PASSWORD…”

Fair concern. So you make that one password a long passphrase, turn on MFA for the vault, and never reuse it anywhere.

The alternative — one weak password reused across 50 sites — is far riskier than one strong vault you actually protect.

Best Practices

The Rules (Actually Good Ones)

✓

Long, not complex

A passphrase of random words beats a short scramble.

✓

Unique per site

One breach shouldn't unlock all your accounts.

✓

MFA on anything important

Email, bank, school, socials — turn it on.

✕

Never store them in Notes

No texts, no docs, no sticky notes. Use a manager.

✕

Stop the forced monthly changes

NIST says only change a password if it's actually leaked.

// source: NIST Digital Identity Guidelines (SP 800-63)
